Five ways to make the event page feel crafted

Each one keeps everything the page does today: the count and its unit, the start dates, the goal, reset with undo, reminders, share, delete, edit and every past run. All three use the add card's language: Allo, the event's own pattern, the floating card for anything you edit, and Torph-style morphing text. Pick a theme at the top and it carries across all three.

What makes today's page feel sloppy

  • System grey boxes and a stock segmented control: it's the only screen that doesn't look like Sinced.
  • The event's own theme never appears, so the page could be any event.
  • Six blocks fight equally for attention: count, reset, undo, reminders, share/delete, runs.
  • Share and Delete in green and red pastel tiles get as much weight as the count.
  • Reset is one tap away with nothing to slow it down; undo comes later as a patch.
  • Edit opens a separate mode, while the add card edits in place.

What all three fix

  • The event's colour and pattern lead the page.
  • One primary action. A and C use hold to reset (deliberate, no accidents); B confirms in the floating card, so you can backdate and add a note on the way.
  • Every editor is the add card's floating card, growing out of what you tapped.
  • Numbers and words morph when they change, never snap.
  • History is something you can touch: a strip, a story or a chart.
